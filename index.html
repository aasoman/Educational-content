💡Core Definitions: Systems, Social Systems & Institutions

1. What is a System?

A system is an interconnected set of elements (people, technologies, rules, environments) that function as a cohesive whole. Its overall behavior is non-linear and cannot be understood by analyzing isolated parts; system behavior emerges from continuous feedback loops and interdependencies.

2. What is a Social System?

A social system consists of human relationships, cultural norms, communication networks, and self-organizing structures. Unlike deterministic technical systems (which produce predictable outputs from inputs), social systems are contingent, evolutionary, and operate on human agency, motivation, and informal adaptations.

3. What is an Institution?

In Ostrom's IAD theory, an institution is not a physical building, but the set of shared prescriptions, rules, norms, and strategies that humans use to organize repetitive, structured interactions. We contrast Formal Rules (written policies/code) with Rules-in-Use (de facto practices on the ground).

CHAPTER 1 • CRITICAL THEORY & ONTOLOGICAL DESIGN

The Illusion of the Isolated Touchpoint

Designers are often trained to focus strictly on form, aesthetics, and usability—designing a hammer or a smartphone interface as an isolated "thing". However, designed artifacts do not exist in a vacuum. They are created by powerful industrial and digital institutions to structure human behavior. When we design digital touchpoints, we dictate how information flows, who receives resources, and who is penalized. Turning our gaze inward as self-critique (Mareis et al.) allows us to unmask how automated platforms perpetuate systemic precarity and harm marginalized populations.

CHAPTER 2 • INSTITUTIONAL ANALYSIS AND DEVELOPMENT (IAD)

Mapping the Action Arena

To diagnose systemic complexity, we treat the policy or platform as an Action Arena (Vincent and Elinor Ostrom). The Action Arena is the focal space where participants holding specific positions take actions leading to outcomes.

[Exogenous Variables: Biophysical/Material Conditions, Attributes of Community, Rules-in-Use] → [Action Arena: Action Situations & Participants] → [Interactions] → [Outcomes & Evaluative Criteria]
CHAPTER 3 • APPLIED DIAGNOSIS & CONFLICTING RULES

When Policy Clashes with Reality

Systemic friction occurs when Formal Rules (the official system mandate or code) clash with Rules-in-Use (how people actually behave to survive).

FIGURE 2: Applied IAD Diagnostic Case Study — Soil & Water Conservation (SWC)
[Top-down State & Donor Pressure] → [Rigid Compulsory Labor Rules] → [Local Landless Farmers Squeezed] → [Low Local Ownership & Unsustainable Maintenance]
CHAPTER 4 • WICKED PROBLEMS & CAUSAL LOOP DIAGRAMS

The Wickedness of Feedback Loops

Public policies and platform economies are Wicked Problems (Jon Kolko, Rittel & Webber)—they have no definitive formulation and no simple "solution". Instead of guessing cause-and-effect, we model system feedback using Causal Loop Diagrams (CLDs) to locate reinforcing loops (vicious cycles) and identify high-leverage intervention points.

FIGURE 3: Dynamics of Feedback Loops — Employee Commitment Virtuous & Vicious Circles
[Reinforcing Loop (R): Initial Algorithmic Delay Penalty] → [Driver Takes Dangerous Shortcuts] → [Accidents Increase] → [System Imposes Stricter Rules] → [Vicious Cycle Escalates]
CHAPTER 5 • SAFETY-II & META-DESIGN GOVERNANCE

Locating Pockets of Resilience

Traditional Safety-I suppresses human workarounds as "errors". But Safety-II recognizes that systems successfully go right because of human adaptation and variability. Students locate "Pockets of Resilience"—informal workarounds—and construct a Meta-Design Governance Framework ("underdesigning" rules) structured around the 4 Capacities of Resilience (MARL): Monitor, Anticipate, Respond, and Learn.

Semester 7 Studio Group Size: Max 2 Students

Studio Brief: Socio-Technical Governance & Policy Intervention

Student pairs select one platform economy or government digital scheme to analyze as a complex socio-technical system. The goal is to formulate complexity, diagnose rule-in-use frictions, and propose a resilient governance roadmap. NO screen wireframing or UI prototyping is required.
